Our retina plays a vital role in helping us see the world around us. Yet many retinal diseases can develop silently, without noticeable symptoms in the early stages. By the time vision changes become obvious, significant retinal damage may already have occurred.

World Retina Day falls on the last Sunday of September, but your retina needs care all year. Here is why retinal health matters and how regular eye examinations can help protect your vision.

---

## What Is the Retina?

The retina is a thin, light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. It receives light and converts it into signals that are sent to the brain, allowing us to see.

Because the retina is essential for vision, damage to it can lead to blurred vision, distorted vision, blind spots, floaters, or even permanent vision loss.

---

## Common Retinal Conditions

Several conditions can affect the retina, including:

- **[Diabetic retinopathy](/treatments/diabetic-retinopathy-treatment/)**: Diabetes can damage the tiny blood vessels supplying the retina. It may progress without noticeable symptoms in the early stages.
- **Age-related macular degeneration (AMD)**: Affects the macula, the central part of the retina responsible for detailed vision.
- **Retinal vein occlusion**: A blockage in a retinal vein can cause swelling and bleeding inside the eye.
- **Central serous retinopathy (CSR)**: Fluid can collect beneath the retina and affect central vision.
- **[Retinal detachment](/treatments/retinal-detachment-surgery/)**: A serious condition in which the retina separates from its normal position and requires urgent medical attention.
- **Inherited retinal diseases**: Conditions such as retinitis pigmentosa can progressively affect vision.

---

## Retinal Diseases Can Develop Silently

One of the biggest challenges with retinal diseases is that symptoms may not appear until the condition has progressed.

You may have:

- No pain
- No obvious change in vision
- No difficulty with daily activities

…and still have an early retinal problem. This is particularly important for people living with **diabetes, high blood pressure, or age-related eye changes**.

---

## Diabetes and Your Retina

Diabetes is one of the important risk factors for retinal disease. High blood sugar over time can damage the blood vessels in the retina, leading to diabetic retinopathy.

Regular retinal examinations can help detect changes before significant vision loss occurs. If you have diabetes, don't wait for your vision to become blurry before getting your eyes checked.

## Warning Signs You Should Not Ignore

### Get seen immediately

**Go to an eye doctor or an emergency department immediately** if you have:

- Sudden loss of vision
- A dark curtain or shadow in your vision
- New flashes of light
- A sudden increase in floaters

Do not wait for a routine appointment. If the clinic is closed or cannot see you promptly, go to an emergency service.

### Contact your eye doctor right away

Get in touch without delay, especially if the change is new or getting worse, if you notice:

- Sudden or unexplained blurred vision
- Distorted central vision, such as straight lines looking wavy
- Missing areas in your field of vision

## Modern Retinal Screening and Diagnosis

Advances in retinal imaging have made it possible to examine the retina in greater detail.

Depending on your condition, an eye specialist may recommend tests such as:

- **Fundus photography**: Captures detailed images of the retina.
- **[OCT (Optical Coherence Tomography)](/blog/oct-essential-eye-exam-tool/)**: Provides cross-sectional images of the retina and helps identify changes that may not be visible during a routine eye examination.
- **OCTA (Optical Coherence Tomography Angiography)**: Allows detailed assessment of retinal blood vessels without the need for dye injection.

These technologies can support early detection, monitoring, and treatment planning for several retinal conditions.

## When Should You Have Your Retina Checked?

A retinal examination may be especially important if you:

- Have diabetes
- Have high blood pressure
- Are above 40 years of age
- Have a family history of retinal disease
- Have already been diagnosed with a retinal condition

Your eye specialist can recommend the appropriate examination based on your age, medical history, symptoms, and risk factors. New flashes, floaters or other changes in vision should not wait for a routine check: follow the [warning signs](#warning-signs-you-should-not-ignore) above.
